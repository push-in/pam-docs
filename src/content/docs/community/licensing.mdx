PAM, PAM Native, the first-party Composer packages, examples, and publication
metadata are licensed under the **Apache License 2.0**. PAM Desktop keeps the
license declared by its own repository.

The complete `LICENSE`, `NOTICE`, and third-party notice files shipped by each
repository and release control. This page is a practical summary, not legal
advice.

## What Apache 2.0 permits

Subject to the license terms, you may:

- use PAM for personal, internal, commercial, or public applications;
- build and sell proprietary software with PAM;
- modify and redistribute PAM;
- create plugins, components, integrations, hosting, support, and training;
- fork the project; and
- include PAM in a larger product or service.

There is no PAM revenue share, application royalty, company-size limit, or
seat limit in Apache 2.0.

## Your obligations

When you redistribute PAM or a derivative containing PAM code, preserve the
license and required notices, mark significant modifications, and follow the
Apache 2.0 patent and attribution terms. Third-party dependencies retain their
own licenses.

Application code authored against PAM's public APIs belongs to its author and
may use different terms. Generated starter code should retain any notices that
the generated files or their source package explicitly require.

## Older releases

Licenses are granted per released copy. A later license change does not revoke
terms already received with an older release. Consult the `LICENSE` distributed
with the exact version you use.

For unusual redistribution, trademark, or patent questions, rely on the
controlling license text and obtain qualified legal advice.
